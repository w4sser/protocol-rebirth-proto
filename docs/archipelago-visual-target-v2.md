### Pass Z — ground cleanup and rose-lit pines (16 November, draft PR #269)

**Owner decision:** remove bright, near-white ground speckle and try a very restrained pink/lilac *sky reflection* on wind-shaped pines. **This is not a request for full-pink trees, glow, a thick vignette, or a new postprocess pass.**

**Commit-ready implementation brief**
- **Ground color:** lower contrast of tiny exposed mineral/lichen flecks in the existing v2 terrain vertex colors. Keep wide weathered granite masses and muted moss/olive/heather, and preserve the exact ground/coast shared seam.
- **Scattered vegetation:** halve native groundcover per cluster (HIGH 12→6, LOW 6→4), keep seeded groups and reservation exclusions; retain LOW as a deterministic subset of HIGH. Reduce white-looking tiny props (small stones, wildflowers, sea-holly) by roughly **70% in v2 only**; preserve grass, established large stones and the normal game's dressing.
- **Pine lighting:** crown-only, upward-facing **rose-lilac sky tint ≤0.11** over the existing dark-green base, baked into a few shared crown vertex-color geometries. No emission, extra lights, full-tree recolor or outline. Trunks and shadows stay dark; individual foreground fade/restore works identically in HIGH/LOW.
- **Technical acceptance:** default mode unchanged; no new collider; no extra draw call per pine, no per-tree materials; preserve seeded layouts, gameplay readability and the `archVisual=v2` flag.

**Before labeling it worked:** compare the same 844×390 real gameplay camera at spawn, shore and foreground pines in HIGH and LOW to the original visual target. Ensure ground speckles no longer attract attention, the pines remain predominantly dark green, and their rose tint reads as ambient sky light. Run full CI, coastal geometry/seed checks and the occlusion fade/restore test. Physical iPhone/Safari review is still required for default rollout. **No merge or visual-parity claim solely from code/tests.**

**Current state:** implemented in [draft PR #269](https://github.com/w4sser/protocol-rebirth-proto/pull/269); image acceptance pending. This cosmetic pass is independent of the major coastline redesign in draft PR #267.

### What we tried and rejected — do not recreate

1. **Rectangular playable grass plates and perimeter rock rows:** artificial island silhouettes; organic shared coastal profiles replaced them.
2. **Wall-length stone arrangements and smooth geological banks across the island:** became obvious parallel rows or long gray sausages, not Swedish granite.
3. **Tall mountains, berms and faux climbable terrain inside combat space:** operator/AI navigation is X/Z-planar. High rock belongs at the **outer visual boundary**, not on a flat gameplay floor.
4. **Freestanding concrete slabs used as natural cover:** wrong visual language. Concrete belongs only to believable military/utility structures.
5. **Intersecting terrace shells, granite aprons and coplanar transition meshes:** caused or risked z-fighting, especially by shoreline and bridges. Prefer coherent indexed geometry and exact shared seams.
6. **Tweaking only gray/pink tint or repeating radial ridges to hide a weak silhouette:** the recurring retaining-wall problem is primarily **composition and geometry**, not just a material problem.
7. **Rectangular boat placeholders or boats chosen from old rectangular room bounds:** replaced by sculpted hulls and coast-aware water placement; never reintroduce these shortcuts.

These were **unsuccessful visual approaches**, even when their intermediate tests were green. They are not future TODOs.

### Visual contract: locked decisions to preserve

- **The island is the rock.** Natural, low-poly glacial granite is integral to the coast, in asymmetrical, rounded masses separated by actual moss/grass and small sheltered coves. Avoid an uninterrupted gray perimeter retaining wall.
- **Gameplay stays planar.** No new climbable mountains, fake elevated floors or invisible visual cover. Tall rocks may be small or large, with different orientations and heights, but stay beyond the walkable coast. Keep bridge landings clear.
- **A calm Nordic summer night, dark but never hopeless.** Cold blue-gray granite, pale lavender/coral sky reflections, healthy muted green vegetation, quiet Baltic water. No neon and no tropical cyan.
- **Distinct windswept Scots pines** in varied height/silhouette/rotation. An individual tree fades when it blocks the operator, enemy, loot, objective or extraction, then restores. No competitive legacy tall-pine dressing in v2.
- **Open sightlines for combat.** Environment can frame danger, but must not conceal actor movement, bullets, loot cues or artillery telegraphs.
- **No concrete blocks scattered in nature.** Structures are embedded or plausibly placed, not procedural substitutes for stone.

## Visual contract (locked direction)

1. **The island is the rock.** Its silhouette is a continuous, irregular, rounded glacial landform. Prefer organic oval or asymmetric large islands, headlands, scooped coves, short steep rock lips, and smaller sheltered inlets; avoid rectangular grass slabs with perimeter props. Coast, headland and isolated slabs must share one geological language and color family.
2. **Swedish archipelago without national markings.** Smooth glacial granite, wind-shaped coastal pines, heather, moss, grasses and quiet Baltic shore. Civilian/Cold War structures are weathered and partly buried in granite. No flags, crowns, emblems or explicit geography labels.
3. **Nordic summer night.** Lavender and pale coral in indirect light and water reflection, cold blue-grey shadows, muted teal shallows, subdued healthy olive/moss interiors. No purple grass, glowing rocks, neon cyberpunk, dystopian orange skies or monochrome gloom. The visual contract remains `Dark, but never hopeless`.
4. **Authored-feeling composition.** 1–3 major silhouettes per island, then middle forms, then sparse ground cover. Clusters, open view corridors, and approximately 35% readily readable walkable area. Nature is irregular; engineered concrete is geometric.
5. **Distinctive coastal defenses.** Sealed portals and pillboxes belong in/against granite slopes, not floating in the sea or scattered as freestanding props. They are non-interactive scenery unless separate gameplay work approves them.
6. **Mobile readability first.** Never bury the Operator, BIT, enemies, projectiles, loot, extraction markers, passages or combat telegraphs behind foliage or cosmetic rocks. Cyan/system, amber/reward and coral/danger color semantics are unchanged.
